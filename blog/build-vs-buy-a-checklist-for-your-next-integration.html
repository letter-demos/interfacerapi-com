Build vs. buy: a checklist for your next integration

Every engineering team eventually faces the question of whether to build an integration in-house. Sometimes building is the right call. If an integration is your core differentiator, owning it end to end makes sense.

For everything else, ask five questions. How many customers will use it? How often does the vendor change its API? Who will be on call when it breaks? Does it need webhooks the vendor does not provide? And how many more integrations like this are coming next year?

Most teams underestimate the maintenance cost. The first version takes weeks, but keeping it healthy through pagination changes, deprecations, and credential issues is a permanent tax of a few engineer-days every month.

If the honest answers point to a long tail of integrations, a unified API is usually cheaper. Reserve your engineers for the work only your team can do.
