Why token refresh is the silent killer of integrations

When we analyzed support tickets across our first hundred customers, we expected most failures to come from vendors changing their APIs. The real top cause was far less exciting: expired or revoked credentials. A refresh token that silently stopped working three weeks ago accounts for more lost data than any breaking change.

The trouble is that the failure is quiet. A sync that returns a 401 once looks like a blip. By the time a customer notices missing records, days of data may be stale, and your team is debugging a problem with no obvious cause.

Our approach is to treat credential health as a first-class signal. Auth Vault refreshes tokens ahead of expiry, tracks each connection's status, and alerts you the moment a user needs to reconnect, before their data goes stale.

If you build this yourself, at minimum log every authentication failure separately from other errors, and surface broken connections to the end user inside your product. A simple banner that says reconnect your account will save you dozens of support threads.
