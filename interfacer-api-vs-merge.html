Interfacer API vs Merge

Both are unified APIs: your product calls one interface, and the vendor deals with every third-party service behind it. They differ in where they focus. Interfacer is built around the plumbing that breaks integrations in production: expiring credentials, flaky webhooks and requests you can't see.

Where Interfacer is the better fit

Credentials you can trust

Most broken integrations aren't vendor API changes. They're tokens that expired quietly. Interfacer refreshes tokens automatically and alerts you when a credential is about to expire or has broken.

Webhooks that don't vanish

Retries with backoff, a point where we stop and dead-letter the event, and replay when you're ready. A vendor outage becomes an afternoon of replayed events, not lost records.

Prices on the page

Starter and Team plans are published, so you can estimate cost before you talk to anyone. See pricing.

Where Merge may suit you better

If your roadmap includes AI agents acting in third-party tools or routing across LLM providers, Merge sells products for that and we don't. Its higher plans also list configurable sync frequency, go-live support packages, and single-tenant and self-hosted options available for purchase. If those matter more than webhook delivery and published pricing, put Merge on your shortlist.

And when to build it yourself

If an integration is your core differentiator, build it in-house and own every detail. For the long tail of CRMs, ledgers and HR systems your customers happen to use, a unified API is usually cheaper than the permanent tax of maintaining each one.
