Interfacer API vs Apideck

Both are unified APIs that let your product talk to many third-party tools through one integration. Apideck leads with accounting and ERP. Interfacer API covers 300+ connectors across CRMs, accounting, ticketing and HR, with credential health and replayable webhooks built in.

The short version

Choose Interfacer API if

  • Your customers use a wide mix of CRMs, accounting tools, ticketing systems and HR platforms
  • You want failed webhook deliveries retried for up to 72 hours, then parked in a dead-letter queue you can replay
  • You want broken credentials flagged in Slack or email before your customers notice stale data
  • You prefer published flat plan prices

Apideck may suit you if

  • Accounting and ERP is your main integration need: Apideck lists 55+ accounting and ERP platforms
  • You want a provider that says it does not store third-party data and processes calls in real time
  • You want per-active-customer pricing and a branded integrations marketplace page

Where we think the difference shows

Most integrations don't die from a vendor changing their API. They die quietly when a token expires or a customer revokes access, and nobody notices until the data is a week stale. That's why Interfacer refreshes tokens automatically, scores the health of every connection and sends Slack or email alerts the moment credentials break.

The same goes for events. Webhooks fail. Ours retry with exponential backoff for up to 72 hours, and anything that still doesn't land goes to a dead-letter queue you can redeliver with one click, instead of disappearing.

What Interfacer API is not

Interfacer API is a developer tool you build into your product. It is not an end-user automation tool like Zapier or a no-code workflow builder. And if a particular integration is the thing that sets your product apart, building it in-house can be the right call. A unified API earns its keep on the long tail.
